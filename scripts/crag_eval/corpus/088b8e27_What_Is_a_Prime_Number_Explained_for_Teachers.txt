A prime number is a whole number greater than 1 with only two factors – itself and 1. A prime number cannot be divided by any other positive integers without leaving a remainder, decimal, or fraction. An example of a prime number is 13. Its only divisors are 1 and 13. Dividing a prime number by another natural number results in numbers leftover. For example, 13 ÷ 6 = 2 remainder 1. 15 is not an example of a prime number because it can be divided by 5 and 3 as well as by itself and 1.
